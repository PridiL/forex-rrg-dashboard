Why not myfxbook data. This follows the layout of myfxbook's forex heat map, but its prices cannot come from there: every myfxbook page and data feed has answered with a bot challenge since early September, the heat map included, and the only price feed it did offer was weekly. The prices here are OANDA's, read from TradingView, for the same eight currencies.

Heat map. Each cell is the row currency priced in the column currency, as a % change over the selected period, so the grid is mirror-imaged across the diagonal. Blue is up, red is down, gray is flat; the scale runs to the largest move on screen, and every cell prints its value. Currency strength is each currency against the other seven at once — the geometric mean of its seven rates — which is the right answer to “who moved”, where a single pair only ever says which of two moved more.

Rotation. Currencies plots those eight strength indices against the equal-weighted basket of all eight. That basket is exactly 1 by construction — every cross adds +log rate to one index and −log rate to another — so there is no benchmark of its own to tug the marks, and no weighting to argue about. Pairs plots each cross against that same unit, which is simply its own price: Leading means above its recent norm and still climbing. Filter to one currency to see its seven pairs; hollow marks are pairs where it is the quote leg, and they rotate opposite to its strength. RS-Ratio is 100 + the z-score over 60 bars; RS-Momentum is 100 + the z-score of its 10-bar change. This is a standard z-score replication of the proprietary JdK formulas: quadrant and direction track the original, exact values will not — treat anything within ~0.3 of a boundary as unclassified.

Completed candles only. The page refreshes five minutes after each 4H candle closes, so the 4H view moves every refresh and the Daily view moves once a day, when the FX day closes at 17:00 New York. The candle still forming is never plotted. Daily candles are labelled by the trading day they cover, which is one day later than the date TradingView stamps on them.

Not advice. A screening view of relative strength, not a recommendation to trade.
