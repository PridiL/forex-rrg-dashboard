The head is a bar still forming. On Daily, 4H, 1H the newest bar has not closed yet, so the head will move before it settles — Daily head 2026-08-27, last close 2026-08-26; 4H head 2026-08-27 05:00 UTC, last close 2026-08-27 01:00 UTC; 1H head 2026-08-27 07:00 UTC, last close 2026-08-27 06:00 UTC. That is what TradingView's own RRG plots, so it is kept here; rebuild with --closed-only for a chart that uses completed bars only. The shorter the timeframe the less this matters — an unfinished hour moves a 1H head far less than an unfinished day moves the Daily one.

Which mode answers which question. Pairs vs DXY ranks tradable instruments against the dollar index. Currencies vs basket ranks the eight currencies themselves. They are not two views of one thing, and the second is not a tidier version of the first — use the pairs mode to pick an instrument, the currencies mode to ask who is actually strong.

The distortion in Pairs vs DXY. Every pair is scored as pair ÷ dollar index, exactly as briefed, and the result is internally consistent — but the dollar enters twice for some pairs and not at all for others. For a USD-quoted major like EURUSD, rising means the dollar is falling, and dividing by DXY — also falling — amplifies the move, so EURUSD, GBPUSD, AUDUSD and NZDUSD tend to cluster and to sit further from the centre than the crosses. For a USD-based major like USDJPY the two dollar legs largely cancel, leaving something closer to "yen versus the dollar basket". For a cross like EURJPY the dollar is not in the pair at all, so DXY contributes pure outside variance. Read positions within a group of similarly-constructed pairs; do not read EURUSD's distance from centre against EURJPY's as if they meant the same thing. The currencies mode has none of this asymmetry.

How the currency indices are built. Each currency's index is the geometric mean of its exchange rate against the other seven. Every rate exists because this universe is the complete G10 matrix — C/X is either the pair CX or the reciprocal of XC — so nothing is proxied or interpolated. The benchmark is the geometric mean of all eight indices, and that basket is identically 1: each ordered pair contributes +log rate to one index and −log rate to the other, so the double sum cancels exactly. An equal-weighted geometric basket of the same eight currencies is their numeraire. Two consequences worth having: the eight marks are guaranteed to spread around the centre with no benchmark of their own tugging them, and there is no weighting choice to argue about. The build divides by the computed basket rather than assuming the identity, and aborts if it drifts from 1 — that would mean a hole in the matrix or an inverted leg, which would leave every mark wrong but still plausible-looking.

One property worth knowing: OANDA quotes each cross independently, so the 28 rates do not triangulate perfectly — AUDCHF differs from AUDUSD × USDCHF by a median of 0.004% and at worst 0.10%. Averaging all seven rates diffuses that noise instead of inheriting it, which a chained construction (routing every currency through the dollar) would not do. At the scale of a 60-bar z-score it is immaterial either way, but it is the reason the indices are built from all seven legs rather than from a single pivot.

Two caveats on the currency mode. This basket is equal-weighted, not trade-weighted, so it is a clean measure of relative currency performance and not a measure of economic competitiveness. And it is not DXY: DXY is 57.6% euro and includes the Swedish krona, which is not in this universe, so the USD mark here will not track the DXY line on your chart. Index levels in the table are rebased to 100 at the start of each view's loaded history, so they compare within a column, not across timeframes.

Panels. In pairs mode: all pairs, then one per currency (USD · EUR · GBP · JPY · CHF · CAD · AUD · NZD). The watchlist turns out to carry the complete G10 matrix — all C(8,2) = 28 combinations, no gaps — so each currency panel holds exactly 7 pairs, and that same completeness is what makes the currency indices constructible. Within a currency panel, side matters: filled marks are pairs where that currency is the base, hollow marks where it is the quote, and hollow marks rotate opposite to the currency's own strength. Currencies mode is a single plot of 8 marks with nothing to facet, so the panel tabs are hidden there.

Method. RS = 100 × (pair ÷ benchmark) on shared bar timestamps. RS-Ratio = 100 + the z-score of RS over a 60-bar window; RS-Momentum = 100 + the z-score of the 10-bar rate of change of RS-Ratio, same window. JdK's published RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum formulas are proprietary, so this is a standard z-score replication — quadrant placement and rotation direction track the original closely, absolute values will not match exactly. Treat anything within ~0.3 of a boundary as unclassified.

Timeframes. Daily (12 weeks), 4H (10 trading days), 1H (2.5 trading days). The window stays 60 bars and the momentum span 10 bars on all three, which is RRG convention — so what changes between views is not the formula but its calendar reach. Read them as different questions, not as confirmation of each other: Daily is a position read, 4H a swing read, and 1H a timing overlay whose 60-bar window sees barely two and a half trading days. A pair can sit deep in Leading on Daily and in Lagging on 1H with nothing contradictory about it.

Bar timestamps. Two feed quirks are corrected here, both of which quietly damage the result if taken at face value. TradingView stamps a daily FX bar at the moment the session opens — 21:00 UTC, 17:00 New York — so its own date label sits one day behind the trading day: the bar it calls Thursday is Friday's session, and the one it calls Sunday is Monday's. Dates here are the corrected trading days. And 4H bars from different feeds sit on different phase grids — OANDA's buckets fall on 01/05/09/13/17/21 UTC, TVC:DXY's on 23/03/07/11/15/19, two hours apart, with the same six-bars-a-day cadence. Joining those raw keeps only 22% of bars, fewer than one RRG point needs, so the 4H view is aggregated from 1H onto a single grid instead of pulled natively. That is exact rather than approximate — a 4H close is the last 1H close in its bucket — and it reproduces OANDA's own 4H closes to the final decimal place. The 1H and Daily views need no such treatment.

Universe. Drawn from watchlist 22377480 (169 rows). Kept: bare six-letter tickers whose halves are both G10 currencies. Dropped: indices, currency indices (DXY, JXY, the WCU basket rows), metals, and every arithmetic expression row. The same pair appears under up to three feeds — OANDA, FX and FX_IDC — and collapses to one mark, preferring OANDA.

Marks share one colour by design: quadrant is carried by position and by the labelled regions, because the Leading↔Lagging colour pair fails colourblind separation (ΔE 4.1 under deuteranopia). Base/quote side is carried by shape for the same reason.
